# Subject-Verb Agreement and Articles

Two small areas of grammar cause a surprisingly large share of errors in student writing: making the verb agree with its subject, and choosing between *a*, *an*, *the* and no article at all. These errors rarely stop a reader from understanding you, but they make writing look careless, especially in applications, emails and exams. This chapter gives you clear rules and plenty of examples for both.

## Subject-Verb Agreement

The basic rule is simple: **a singular subject takes a singular verb, and a plural subject takes a plural verb.**

- "The student *writes*." (singular)
- "The students *write*." (plural)

In the simple present, singular third-person verbs end in *-s* (*writes, goes, has*), while plural verbs do not. Notice the pattern: nouns add *-s* to become plural, but verbs add *-s* to become singular.

With *be*: *I am*, *he/she/it is*, *we/you/they are*; *I/he/she/it was*, *we/you/they were*.

> **Key idea:** Find the true subject first, then match the verb to it. Most agreement errors happen because a nearby word distracts you from the real subject.

## Tricky Agreement Rules

### 1. Words Between the Subject and the Verb

Ignore phrases that come between the subject and the verb.

- "The **box** of chocolates *is* on the table." (subject: box)
- "The **quality** of the products *has* improved." (subject: quality)
- "One of my friends *is* a doctor." (subject: one)

Phrases such as *along with*, *as well as*, *together with* and *in addition to* do not change the subject:

- "The **teacher**, along with her students, *is* going on the trip."

### 2. Subjects Joined by And

Two subjects joined by *and* are usually plural:

- "Riya and Meera *are* classmates."

But if the two words name one idea or one person, use singular:

- "Bread and butter *is* my usual breakfast."
- "The secretary and treasurer *has* resigned." (one person holding both roles)

### 3. Or, Nor, Either...Or, Neither...Nor

The verb agrees with the subject **closer** to it:

- "Either the students or the teacher *is* responsible."
- "Neither the teacher nor the students *are* responsible."

### 4. Each, Every, Either, Neither and Indefinite Pronouns

These words are singular:

- "**Each** of the players *has* a jersey."
- "**Every** student *needs* an ID card."
- "**Everyone** *is* welcome."
- "**Neither** of the answers *is* correct."

Also singular: *anyone, anybody, someone, somebody, no one, nobody, everything, nothing*.

Plural: *both, few, many, several*. "Both *are* ready." "Many *were* absent."

Singular or plural depending on what they refer to: *all, some, most, none, any, half*.

- "Some of the cake *is* left." (cake is uncountable)
- "Some of the cakes *are* left." (cakes is plural)

### 5. Collective Nouns

Collective nouns (*team, family, committee, audience, class, government*) usually take a **singular** verb when the group acts as one unit, especially in Indian and American English:

- "The team *is* winning."
- "The committee *has* made its decision."

When members act individually, a plural verb can be used, especially in British English: "The family *are* arguing among themselves." When in doubt, use singular, or rephrase: "The family members are arguing."

### 6. Amounts, Distances and Time

Quantities treated as one unit are singular:

- "Five hundred rupees *is* too much for this book."
- "Ten kilometres *is* a long walk."
- "Two hours *is* enough for the exam."

### 7. Nouns That Look Plural

Some nouns end in *-s* but are singular:

- Subjects of study: *mathematics, physics, economics*. "Economics *is* my favourite subject."
- *news*: "The news *is* good."
- Diseases and games: *measles, billiards*.

Some nouns are always plural: *scissors, trousers, jeans, spectacles, goods, belongings*. "My jeans *are* torn." But "A pair of jeans *is* on sale." (subject: pair)

### 8. There Is / There Are

The verb agrees with the noun that *follows*:

- "There *is* a mistake in your answer."
- "There *are* three mistakes in your answer."

### 9. The Number vs A Number

- "**The number** of students *has* increased." (singular: the number itself)
- "**A number** of students *have* complained." (plural: several students)

### 10. Relative Clauses

In a relative clause, the verb agrees with the noun the relative pronoun refers to:

- "She is one of those students who *work* very hard." (*who* refers to *students*)
- "She is the only one of the students who *works* part-time." (*who* refers to *the only one*)

## Articles

English has two kinds of articles:

- the **indefinite articles** *a* and *an*, used for something not specific, and
- the **definite article** *the*, used for something specific or already known.

### A or An?

The choice depends on **sound**, not spelling.

- Use *a* before a consonant sound: *a book, a university* (you-niversity), *a one-way street* (won), *a European* (you-ropean).
- Use *an* before a vowel sound: *an apple, an hour* (silent h), *an honest man, an MBA* (em-bee-ay), *an X-ray* (ex-ray).

### When to Use A/An

1. With a singular countable noun mentioned for the first time: "I saw *a* dog in the park."
2. To mean "one": "I need *a* pen."
3. With jobs: "She is *an* engineer." (Many Indian speakers drop this article; "She is engineer" is incorrect.)
4. In expressions of rate: "twice *a* day", "fifty rupees *a* kilo".
5. In exclamations: "What *a* lovely day!"

### When to Use The

1. When something has already been mentioned: "I saw a dog. *The* dog was barking."
2. When the listener knows which one you mean: "Please close *the* door." (the door of this room)
3. With unique things: *the sun, the moon, the internet, the world*.
4. With superlatives and ordinals: *the best, the tallest, the first, the last*.
5. With rivers, oceans, seas, mountain ranges and deserts: *the Ganga, the Arabian Sea, the Himalayas, the Thar Desert*.
6. With plural country names and names containing *republic*, *kingdom* or *states*: *the Netherlands, the United States, the United Kingdom*.
7. With musical instruments: "She plays *the* violin."
8. With a singular noun representing a whole class: "*The* tiger is an endangered animal."
9. With famous buildings, newspapers and some organisations: *the Taj Mahal, the Hindu, the United Nations*.
10. With *only, same, next* (in a sequence): "*the* only option", "*the* same answer".

### When to Use No Article (the Zero Article)

1. With plural and uncountable nouns used in a general sense: "*Dogs* are loyal." "*Water* is essential." "*Honesty* is important."
2. With most names of people, cities, countries and continents: *Priya, Mumbai, India, Asia*.
3. With single mountains and lakes: *Mount Everest, Lake Dal*.
4. With meals, in general: "We had *lunch* at one."
5. With languages and subjects: "She speaks *French*." "I study *physics*."
6. With days, months and festivals: "on *Monday*", "in *July*", "during *Diwali*".
7. With some places when we mean their main purpose: "go to *school*", "go to *bed*", "in *hospital*" (as a patient), "at *college*". Compare: "My father went to *the school* to meet my teacher." (visiting the building, not to study)

> **Key idea:** Ask two questions. Is the noun countable and singular? Then it needs *a/an*, *the* or another determiner. Is it specific and known to the reader? Then use *the*.

### Common Article Errors

| Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|
| He is best student in class. | He is **the** best student in **the** class. |
| I am going to the office by the bus. | I am going to the office by bus. |
| She is a honest person. | She is **an** honest person. |
| The life is beautiful. | Life is beautiful. |
| I want to become doctor. | I want to become **a** doctor. |
| The India is a large country. | India is a large country. |
| He plays guitar and the cricket. | He plays **the** guitar and cricket. |

Note: we use no article with sports ("play cricket") but *the* with musical instruments ("play the guitar").

## Putting It Together: An Editing Exercise

Original:

> "The number of student in the college have increased. Each of them need a ID card, and the committee are deciding a new design. A information will be sent on the Monday."

Corrected:

> "The number of students in the college **has** increased. Each of them **needs an** ID card, and the committee **is** deciding **on** a new design. **The** information will be sent on Monday."

## Summary

- A singular subject takes a singular verb; a plural subject takes a plural verb. Find the true subject first.
- Ignore words between subject and verb; *along with* and *as well as* do not make a subject plural.
- With *or* and *nor*, match the closer subject; *each*, *every* and most indefinite pronouns are singular.
- Collective nouns and amounts are usually singular; *news* and subjects like *economics* are singular.
- Choose *a* or *an* by sound; use *the* for specific, known or unique things.
- Use no article for general plurals and uncountables, most proper names, meals, languages, days and sports.

## Practice Questions

1. Choose the correct verb: "Neither the manager nor the employees (was / were) informed."
2. Correct the sentence: "One of the boys have lost their bag."
3. Fill in *a*, *an*, *the* or leave blank: "___ Ganga is ___ longest river in ___ India, and ___ hour-long boat ride on it is ___ unforgettable experience."
4. Explain the difference between "go to school" and "go to the school".
5. Write three sentences using *the number of* and *a number of* correctly.
