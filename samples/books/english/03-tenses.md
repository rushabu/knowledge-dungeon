# Tenses

Tense tells us **when** something happens: in the past, the present or the future. English also uses **aspect** to show whether an action is simple, ongoing (continuous), completed (perfect) or ongoing up to a point (perfect continuous). Three times combined with four aspects give the twelve tenses. They look like a lot, but they follow a clear system.

## The Big Picture

| | Simple | Continuous | Perfect | Perfect continuous |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Present** | I work | I am working | I have worked | I have been working |
| **Past** | I worked | I was working | I had worked | I had been working |
| **Future** | I will work | I will be working | I will have worked | I will have been working |

> **Key idea:** Continuous tenses use *be* + *-ing*. Perfect tenses use *have* + past participle. Perfect continuous tenses use *have been* + *-ing*. Once you know these building blocks, you can build any tense.

## Verb Forms You Need

Every verb has these forms:

- **Base form:** *go, write, play*
- **Past simple:** *went, wrote, played*
- **Past participle:** *gone, written, played*
- **Present participle (-ing form):** *going, writing, playing*

Regular verbs add *-ed* for both past forms (*play, played, played*). Irregular verbs must be learnt:

| Base | Past | Past participle |
|---|---|---|
| begin | began | begun |
| break | broke | broken |
| bring | brought | brought |
| choose | chose | chosen |
| do | did | done |
| eat | ate | eaten |
| forget | forgot | forgotten |
| go | went | gone |
| know | knew | known |
| teach | taught | taught |
| write | wrote | written |

## Present Tenses

### Simple Present

**Form:** base verb, adding *-s/-es* for *he, she, it*: *I work, she works*.

**Uses:**

- Habits and routines: "She *goes* to the gym every morning."
- General truths and facts: "Water *boils* at 100 degrees Celsius."
- Timetables and schedules: "The train *leaves* at 6:15."
- Permanent situations: "My parents *live* in Nagpur."

**Negatives and questions** use *do/does*: "He *does not play*." "*Does* he *play*?" Note that the main verb returns to the base form: never "does not plays".

### Present Continuous

**Form:** *am/is/are* + *-ing*: "They *are studying*."

**Uses:**

- Actions happening now: "I *am writing* an email."
- Temporary situations: "She *is staying* with her cousin this month."
- Fixed future plans: "We *are meeting* the client tomorrow."
- Changing situations: "Prices *are rising*."

**Stative verbs** describe states, not actions, and are usually *not* used in continuous forms. These include *know, believe, understand, like, love, want, need, own, belong, seem*. Say "I *know* the answer", not "I am knowing the answer"; "I *understand*", not "I am understanding".

### Present Perfect

**Form:** *have/has* + past participle: "She *has finished*."

**Uses:**

- Past actions with a result now: "I *have lost* my keys." (I still cannot find them.)
- Experiences at an unspecified time: "*Have* you ever *been* to Goa?"
- Actions continuing up to now, with *for* or *since*: "We *have known* each other *for* ten years." "He *has worked* here *since* 2022."
- Recent actions with *just, already, yet*: "The results *have just been* announced."

> **Key idea:** Do not use the present perfect with a finished time expression. Say "I *saw* him yesterday", not "I have seen him yesterday". Words like *yesterday, last week, in 2020* and *ago* need the simple past.

### Present Perfect Continuous

**Form:** *have/has been* + *-ing*: "It *has been raining*."

**Uses:** actions that began in the past and are still continuing or have just stopped, emphasising duration: "I *have been waiting* for an hour." "You look tired. *Have* you *been working* late?"

## Past Tenses

### Simple Past

**Form:** past form of the verb: *worked, went*. Negatives and questions use *did* + base form: "She *did not go*." "*Did* you *see* it?"

**Uses:**

- Completed actions at a specific past time: "We *visited* Jaipur last summer."
- A series of past actions: "He *woke up*, *had* breakfast and *left*."
- Past habits: "As a child, I *played* cricket every evening." (Also: "I *used to play*".)

### Past Continuous

**Form:** *was/were* + *-ing*: "They *were watching* TV."

**Uses:**

- An action in progress at a past moment: "At 8 pm I *was having* dinner."
- A background action interrupted by another: "I *was walking* home when it *started* to rain."
- Two actions in progress at the same time: "While she *was cooking*, he *was cleaning*."

### Past Perfect

**Form:** *had* + past participle: "The train *had left*."

**Use:** an action completed *before* another past action or time: "When we reached the station, the train *had* already *left*." It makes the order of past events clear.

### Past Perfect Continuous

**Form:** *had been* + *-ing*.

**Use:** duration of an activity up to a past moment: "She *had been studying* for three hours when the power went out."

## Future Forms

English has several ways to talk about the future, each with a slightly different meaning.

### Will + Base Form

- Predictions: "It *will rain* tomorrow."
- Instant decisions: "The phone is ringing. I*'ll get* it."
- Promises and offers: "I *will help* you with the project."

### Going To

- Plans already decided: "I*'m going to apply* for an internship."
- Predictions based on present evidence: "Look at those clouds. It*'s going to rain*."

### Present Continuous and Simple Present for the Future

- Arrangements: "I*'m meeting* the professor at 3."
- Timetables: "The exam *starts* at 10 am."

### Future Continuous

**Form:** *will be* + *-ing*: "This time tomorrow, I *will be flying* to Delhi." It describes an action in progress at a future time.

### Future Perfect

**Form:** *will have* + past participle: "By next June, I *will have completed* my degree." It describes an action that will be finished before a future time.

### Future Perfect Continuous

**Form:** *will have been* + *-ing*: "By December, she *will have been working* here for five years."

## Time Expressions and Their Tenses

| Expression | Usual tense |
|---|---|
| every day, usually, always, often | Simple present |
| now, at the moment, currently | Present continuous |
| ever, never, just, already, yet, so far | Present perfect |
| for, since (up to now) | Present perfect (continuous) |
| yesterday, last week, ago, in 2019 | Simple past |
| while, when (background action) | Past continuous |
| by the time, before (in the past) | Past perfect |
| tomorrow, next week, soon | Future forms |
| by next year, by then | Future perfect |

## Common Tense Mistakes

1. "I am having two brothers." Correct: "I *have* two brothers." (*have* for possession is stative.)
2. "I have completed my degree in 2023." Correct: "I *completed* my degree in 2023."
3. "She did not went." Correct: "She did not *go*."
4. "I am working here since 2022." Correct: "I *have been working* here since 2022."
5. "If it will rain, we will stay home." Correct: "If it *rains*, we will stay home." (No *will* in the *if* clause; see below.)

## Conditional Sentences

Conditionals use tenses in fixed combinations.

| Type | Meaning | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zero | General truth | If + present, present | If you heat ice, it melts. |
| First | Real future possibility | If + present, will + base | If it rains, we will cancel the trip. |
| Second | Unreal or unlikely present/future | If + past, would + base | If I won the lottery, I would travel the world. |
| Third | Unreal past (regret) | If + past perfect, would have + past participle | If I had studied, I would have passed. |

In the second conditional, formal English uses *were* for all subjects: "If I *were* you, I would apologise."

## Sequence of Tenses

In a complex sentence, the tense of the dependent clause often follows the main clause. If the main verb is in the past, the reported or dependent verb usually shifts to a past form: "She said that she *was* ready." "I thought you *had* left." General truths keep the present: "The teacher said that light *travels* faster than sound."

## Summary

- Twelve tenses come from three times (present, past, future) and four aspects (simple, continuous, perfect, perfect continuous).
- Continuous = *be* + *-ing*; perfect = *have* + past participle.
- Use the simple present for habits and facts, and the present continuous for now and temporary actions; avoid continuous forms with stative verbs.
- Use the present perfect for experiences, results and duration up to now, but never with finished time expressions.
- The past perfect shows which past action happened first.
- Use *will*, *going to* and present forms appropriately for the future.
- Conditionals follow fixed tense patterns; never put *will* in the *if* clause of a first conditional.
