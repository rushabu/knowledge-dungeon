# Building Vocabulary

A rich vocabulary helps you understand what you read, express yourself precisely, and make a strong impression in interviews, emails and exams. Vocabulary is not about using long, rare words to sound clever. It is about having the *right* word available when you need it. This chapter covers how words are built, the relationships between words, commonly confused words, idioms, and practical ways to keep learning.

## How Words Are Built

Many English words are made of smaller meaningful parts. Knowing these parts lets you guess the meaning of thousands of unfamiliar words.

- The **root** carries the core meaning.
- A **prefix** is added before the root and changes its meaning.
- A **suffix** is added after the root and often changes the part of speech.

For example, *unbelievable* = *un-* (not) + *believe* + *-able* (can be): "cannot be believed".

### Common Prefixes

| Prefix | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| un-, in-, im-, il-, ir- | not | unhappy, invisible, impossible, illegal, irregular |
| dis- | opposite, not | disagree, dishonest |
| mis- | wrongly | misunderstand, misspell |
| re- | again | rewrite, reconsider |
| pre- | before | preview, prehistoric |
| post- | after | postpone, postgraduate |
| inter- | between | international, interact |
| sub- | under | submarine, subway |
| super- | above | supervise, supernatural |
| trans- | across | transport, translate |
| co-, con-, com- | together | cooperate, connect, combine |
| anti- | against | antibiotic, antisocial |
| auto- | self | automatic, autobiography |

### Common Suffixes

| Suffix | Forms | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| -tion, -sion, -ment, -ness, -ity | nouns | information, decision, development, kindness, ability |
| -er, -or, -ist | people who do something | teacher, actor, scientist |
| -able, -ible | adjectives: can be done | readable, flexible |
| -ful, -less | adjectives: full of / without | helpful, careless |
| -ous, -ive, -al | adjectives | famous, creative, national |
| -ize / -ise, -ify | verbs | organise, simplify |
| -ly | adverbs | quickly, clearly |

### Common Roots (from Latin and Greek)

| Root | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| bio | life | biology, biography |
| graph, gram | write | autograph, telegram |
| tele | far | telephone, telescope |
| port | carry | transport, portable, export |
| dict | say | predict, dictionary, contradict |

> **Key idea:** When you meet an unknown word, break it into parts and combine that with the context of the sentence. You will often be able to work out the meaning without a dictionary.

## Word Families

Learn words in families rather than alone. From *decide*: *decision* (noun), *decisive* (adjective), *decisively* (adverb), *indecisive* (opposite). From *economy*: *economic*, *economical*, *economist*, *economise*. Note that *economic* means "related to the economy" (economic growth) while *economical* means "saving money" (an economical car).

## Synonyms and Antonyms

**Synonyms** have similar meanings; **antonyms** have opposite meanings.

Synonyms are rarely perfect substitutes. They differ in **strength**, **tone** and **usage**:

- *Big*, *large*, *huge*, *enormous*, *massive*: increasing strength.
- *Thin* (neutral), *slim* (positive), *skinny* (negative): different tones.
- *Start* (everyday), *commence* (formal).

Choosing the right synonym is a mark of good writing. "The committee *commenced* the meeting" suits a formal report; "Let's *start*" suits a chat.

Antonyms are often formed with prefixes (*honest, dishonest*; *legal, illegal*) but many are separate words (*generous, stingy*; *expand, contract*).

## Collocations

**Collocations** are words that naturally go together. Native speakers say "make a decision", not "do a decision". Learning collocations makes your English sound natural.

| Verb | Collocates with |
|---|---|
| make | a decision, a mistake, progress, an effort, a phone call, money |
| do | homework, research, business, your best, a favour |
| take | a break, an exam, notes, a photo, responsibility |
| have | a meeting, lunch, a good time, an argument |
| pay | attention, a visit, a compliment |
| catch | a cold, a bus, someone's attention |

Other common collocations: *heavy rain* (not strong rain), *strong coffee* (not powerful coffee), *highly recommended*, *deeply concerned*, *bitterly disappointed*, *a fast train*, *a quick shower*.

## Commonly Confused Words

| Pair | Meaning and example |
|---|---|
| accept / except | *accept* = receive: "I accept your offer." *except* = excluding: "all except Ravi" |
| affect / effect | *affect* (verb) = influence: "Stress affects sleep." *effect* (noun) = result: "a side effect" |
| advice / advise | *advice* (noun): "good advice". *advise* (verb): "I advise you to rest." |
| lose / loose | *lose* = misplace or not win. *loose* = not tight. |
| principal / principle | *principal* = head of a school, or main. *principle* = a rule or belief. |
| stationary / stationery | *stationary* = not moving. *stationery* = pens and paper. |
| complement / compliment | *complement* = complete or go well with. *compliment* = praise. |
| weather / whether | *weather* = climate conditions. *whether* = if. |
| then / than | *then* = at that time or next. *than* = for comparisons. |
| lie / lay | *lie* = recline (no object): "Lie down." *lay* = put something down: "Lay the book on the table." |
| fewer / less | *fewer* for countable nouns: "fewer students". *less* for uncountable: "less water". |
| borrow / lend | *borrow* = take temporarily: "Can I borrow your pen?" *lend* = give temporarily: "Can you lend me your pen?" |

### Indian English Usages to Check in Formal Writing

Some expressions common in India can confuse international readers:

- "Do the needful": say exactly what is needed, such as "Please send the signed form."
- "Revert back" (to mean reply): use "reply" or "respond".
- "Prepone": widely understood in India, but "bring forward" or "move earlier" is safer internationally.
- "Out of station": "out of town" or "travelling".
- "Passed out of college": "graduated from college" (to *pass out* usually means to faint).
- "Kindly": fine, but do not overuse it; "please" is usually more natural.

## One-Word Substitutions

Replacing a phrase with one precise word makes writing concise.

| Phrase | Word |
|---|---|
| A person who does not believe in God | atheist |
| A government by the people | democracy |
| Something that cannot be read | illegible |
| A person who knows many languages | polyglot |
| Fear of heights | acrophobia |
| A speech made without preparation | extempore |
| One who looks on the bright side | optimist |
| That which cannot be avoided | inevitable |
| A place where birds are kept | aviary |
| Life story written by oneself | autobiography |

## Idioms and Phrasal Verbs

### Idioms

An **idiom** is a phrase whose meaning is different from the meanings of its individual words.

| Idiom | Meaning |
|---|---|
| break the ice | start a conversation in an awkward situation |
| a piece of cake | very easy |
| hit the books | study hard |
| once in a blue moon | very rarely |
| under the weather | slightly ill |
| cost an arm and a leg | be very expensive |
| the ball is in your court | it is your decision now |
| burn the midnight oil | work late into the night |
| on the same page | in agreement |
| get the hang of something | learn how to do something |

Idioms make speech lively but are often too informal for academic writing. Use them in conversation and informal emails.

### Phrasal Verbs

A **phrasal verb** is a verb plus a particle (*up, out, off, on, down*) with a special meaning.

- *look up* (search for information), *look after* (take care of), *look forward to* (anticipate happily)
- *give up* (quit), *give in* (surrender)
- *put off* (postpone), *put up with* (tolerate)
- *carry out* (perform), *find out* (discover), *turn down* (reject)
- *come up with* (think of an idea), *run out of* (have none left)

In formal writing, a single-word verb is often better: *postpone* instead of *put off*, *investigate* instead of *look into*.

## How to Build Your Vocabulary

1. **Read widely and regularly.** Newspapers, novels, magazines and good blogs expose you to words in context. Even 20 minutes a day adds up.
2. **Learn words in context, not lists.** Write down the whole sentence in which you found a new word.
3. **Keep a vocabulary notebook or app.** Record the word, its meaning, part of speech, an example sentence and related words.
4. **Use spaced repetition.** Review new words after one day, three days, a week and a month. Flashcard apps do this automatically, and so does this book's game.
5. **Use new words actively.** Try each word in speech or writing at least three times within a week.
6. **Learn word families and collocations**, not just single meanings.
7. **Play with words:** crosswords, word games and quizzes make practice enjoyable.

> **Key idea:** A word is truly learnt when you can *use* it correctly, not merely recognise it. Active use and spaced review turn passive vocabulary into active vocabulary.

## Summary

- Roots, prefixes and suffixes let you decode unfamiliar words.
- Learn words in families, and notice differences in strength and tone between synonyms.
- Collocations (*make a decision*, *heavy rain*) make English sound natural.
- Master commonly confused pairs such as *affect/effect* and *principal/principle*, and check Indian English usages in formal writing.
- One-word substitutions make writing concise; idioms and phrasal verbs suit informal contexts.
- Build vocabulary through wide reading, context, spaced repetition and active use.

## Practice Questions

1. Break these words into prefix, root and suffix and give their meanings: *transportation*, *unpredictable*, *inspector*.
2. Choose the correct word: "The new policy will (affect / effect) all students, and its (affect / effect) will be seen next year."
3. Fill in *make*, *do* or *take*: "___ a decision", "___ your homework", "___ notes", "___ a mistake".
4. Give one word for: "a person who loves books", "a word opposite in meaning".
5. Rewrite formally: "Please do the needful and revert back by tomorrow."
